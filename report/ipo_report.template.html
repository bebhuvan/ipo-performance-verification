Indian IPOs · January 2020 to October 2026

What an Indian IPO is worth after the listing-day pop

1,515 IPOs on NSE and BSE, every listing price checked against the exchanges' own files. The listing gain is real. What follows it, for most issues, is a slow slide behind the market.

Listing data to 6 October 2026; post-listing prices to 28 September 2026. Main Board and SME are analysed separately; 2026 is year to date.

Update scope: 29 new IPOs have verified listing prices. Market-adjusted listing returns, size groups and post-listing charts retain the original 28 September cohort. Missing values for the new IPOs are not zero returns.

Key findings
  1. The pop is most of the return. The median Main Board IPO closed 12.0% above its issue price on listing day. Someone who bought at that close was, at the median, up just 1.5% two years later, while the Nifty 500 total-return index rose 29.6% over the same stretch.
  2. Only about four in ten IPOs beat the market after listing. From the listing-day close to 28 September 2026, 39.5% of Main Board IPOs and 38.5% of SME IPOs with at least a year of history have outrun the Nifty 500.
  3. The year you list in matters more than anything else we measured. SME IPOs from 2020 and 2021 beat the index; those from 2024 have lost a median 20.6% a year since listing and 57% trade below their issue price.
  4. Recent Main Board issues are underwater. 44% of 2024 IPOs and 58% of 2025 IPOs traded below their issue price on 28 September 2026.
The issuance cycle

IPOs arrive in waves

Main Board listings rose from 14 in 2020 to 104 in 2025. SME listings grew faster, from 30 to 268, and now make up seven in ten of all IPOs. That imbalance is why this report treats the two boards separately: pooled, "the typical Indian IPO" would quietly become an SME IPO.

Part one

Main Board: 463 IPOs

Listing day

The median Main Board IPO closed its first day 12.0% above its issue price, and 72.6% closed above it. The average, 21.2%, is pulled up by a few spectacular debuts: 13% of issues gained more than half, while 27% listed flat or lower.

The market barely moved while these IPOs waited to list. Over the typical five days between offer close and listing, the Nifty 500's median move was 0.1%, so the market-adjusted median gain is almost identical at 12.0%. The pop belongs to the IPO, not to the market.

Good years and weak years differ sharply. Median listing gains were near 20% in 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024, and near 5% in 2022 and 2025.

Most of the gain arrives at the opening trade. The median Main Board IPO opened 10.0% above issue and closed at 12.0%, but 47.7% closed below their own opening price. Chasing a hot listing at the open is close to a coin flip for the rest of the day.

After listing

To avoid mixing different groups of IPOs at different horizons, the chart below follows one fixed group: the 230 Main Board IPOs that have been listed for at least two years. Returns run from the listing-day close and include reinvested dividends; the benchmark is the Nifty 500 total-return index over each IPO's own dates.

The median IPO drifted from −1.8% after a month to +8.5% at one year and back to +1.5% at two years. The index median over the same windows reached 29.6%. The range also widens: by year two, a quarter of these IPOs were down more than 31% and a quarter up more than 49%.

Allottees did better only because they started from the issue price. Measured from issue, 56% of IPOs were ahead of the index after one year. By year two that edge was gone: 47%, below even odds.

By listing year

Every Main Board listing year from 2020 to 2025 has trailed the market since listing, at the median by 5 to 9 percentage points a year. The share of IPOs ahead of the index ranges from 29% (2020) to 45% (2022).

By company size

Size uses SEBI's definition through AMFI's half-yearly rankings: the top 100 companies by market capitalisation are large caps, 101 to 250 mid caps, the rest small caps. We call companies outside the top 500 micro caps. Each IPO's value is its post-issue share count at the issue price, compared with the AMFI list in force when it listed.

Two-thirds of Main Board IPOs are micro caps by this measure. The 15 large-cap IPOs listed with a median gain of just 1.9%, and a year later 10 of the 11 old enough to measure were behind the index. Mid caps did best on both counts. With 15 and 30 companies in the two largest groups, read these as tendencies, not rules.

Part two

SME: 1,052 IPOs

Listing day

The median SME IPO closed 9.8% above issue, and 74.6% closed above it. The spread is much wider than on the Main Board: 24% of SME issues gained more than half on day one, and 5% more than doubled.

The yearly pattern is extreme. In 2020 and 2021 the median SME listing gained 1–2%. In 2024 it gained 39.4%, and nine in ten closed above issue. By 2025 and 2026 the median was back near 4%.

The median SME IPO opened 7.1% above issue and closed at 9.8%; 37.5% closed below their opening price.

One pattern changes how the recent years should be read. Since January 2025, no SME IPO in this data has opened more than 90% above its issue price, and 33 opened at exactly +90%. Many of those then rose the 5% daily limit to close near +99.5%, the flat line visible in the chart above. Before 2025, 60 SME IPOs opened higher than +90%, one as high as +387%. This points to a cap on SME opening prices, so the 2025 and 2026 listing gains are not directly comparable with 2024's.

After listing

For the 555 SME IPOs listed at least two years, the median return from the listing-day close was −1.4% after a month, +6.1% at one year and +12.4% at two years, against a median Nifty 500 move of 33.6%. SME returns are price returns; almost no SME company pays a dividend, so the comparison uses the Nifty 500 price index.

The spread is the real story. At two years, a quarter of these IPOs were down more than 49% and a quarter up more than 122%. The middle is a poor guide to what any single SME IPO will do.

By listing year

Listing year splits SME outcomes cleanly. The 2020 and 2021 listings caught the 2023–24 small-company rally: 57% and 62% of them are ahead of the index. Listings from 2022 and 2023 roughly broke even while the index made about 10% a year. The 2024 class, which had the biggest listing gains, has since lost a median 20.6% a year, and 57% trade below issue.

Part three

The two boards side by side

SME IPOs pop about as much as Main Board IPOs at the median, but far more often by extreme amounts, and their outcomes after listing are more spread out in both directions. On the one number that matters for a long-term holder, the odds of beating the index after listing, the two boards end up in the same place: a little under four in ten.

How this was built

Methodology and coverage

  • Universe. 1,515 IPOs listed on NSE or BSE from 1 January 2020 to 6 October 2026: 463 Main Board, 1,052 SME. The 29 additions since 28 September are included in listing statistics only; size, market adjustment and post-listing results cover the original 1,486 IPOs. REITs, InvITs and follow-on offers are excluded. The list was built from exchange issue records, cross-checked against an independent listing dataset, and cleaned of 12 duplicate or non-listed records. It is not certified as every Indian IPO in the period.
  • Listing-day prices. Every listing-day close was matched to the official NSE or BSE bhavcopy for that date. The original cohort was also cross-checked against Kite historical candles; the 29 additions have exchange checks only. We use the NSE close where the IPO traded on NSE and the BSE close otherwise. Listing return is close ÷ issue price − 1, unadjusted.
  • Market adjustment on listing day. The Nifty 500's move from the offer-close date to the listing date. 83 offer-close dates were estimated from the opening date (80) or the T+3 rule (3). These comparisons cover 450 Main Board and 1,036 SME IPOs through 28 September; the 29 new listings have no benchmark or size estimates yet.
  • After listing. Daily closes from official bhavcopies on both exchanges. Horizons are 21, 63, 126, 252 and 504 exchange trading days. Splits, bonuses and consolidations come from official exchange corporate-action records, each confirmed by the actual price move on its ex-date; rights issues use the theoretical ex-rights price. Main Board returns reinvest dividends and are compared with the Nifty 500 TRI; SME returns are price returns compared with the Nifty 500 price index, since SME dividends are negligible.
  • No survivorship bias. Every IPO stays in the numbers. 38 SME stocks that stopped trading or trade rarely are held at their last traded price. Treating those as total losses would lower the SME results further.
  • Size. Post-issue shares × issue price, compared with the AMFI half-yearly ranking in force at listing. Share counts come from NSE's listing-day market-capitalisation file (February 2024 onward), from exchange listing notices for boundary cases, and otherwise from share counts traced back from February 2024 through splits and bonuses.
  • Not included. Money raised and money left on the table (issue-size records need their own verification), subscription data, sector, and any trading strategy. Past returns here describe what happened; they are not a forecast.

Sources: NSE and BSE bhavcopies, listing notices and corporate-action records; AMFI market-capitalisation lists; Nifty Indices; Zerodha Kite Connect historical data.
